*By TruCount CPA  PC, Accounting, tax and advisory firm in South Dakota, serving clients in the Brookings area and throughout the Midwest*

Many families think long-term care is something you deal with when it happens. By then, the options left are usually the most expensive ones. Long-term care is a financial risk, not just a health event, and it deserves the same planning you'd give [retirement savings or a farm succession plan](/resources/long-term-care-how-you-can-financially-prepare-for-the-expen-2).

The numbers are bigger than most people expect. A private nursing home room in South Dakota commonly runs $8,000 to $10,000 a month, and assisted living in the Brookings area often lands between $3,500 and $5,500. Two years of that is a six-figure expense Medicare does not cover beyond a short post-hospital stay. Medicaid can help, but only after most assets are spent down, which is the outcome most families want to avoid.

## Why waiting until you need it is the most expensive option

Long-term care insurance is priced on age and health at the time you apply. A healthy 55-year-old pays substantially less than a 65-year-old with early signs of hypertension or diabetes, and someone who waits until a diagnosis may not qualify at all.

Self-funding is the other end of the spectrum. Some people earmark investments for care rather than pay premiums for coverage they may never use. This can work, but it takes a big enough asset base that a $200,000 to $400,000 care event doesn't derail a spouse's retirement or a farm's operating capital. When land and equipment make up most of the balance sheet, that takes real planning.

## Comparing your funding options side by side

| Funding method | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Long-term care insurance | Clients in their 50s to early 60s in good health | Premiums can rise over time; underwriting gets stricter with age |
| VA Aid and Attendance benefit | Wartime veterans and surviving spouses needing help with daily activities | Requires meeting service and net worth criteria; application process takes months |
| Self-funded savings or investments | Clients with significant liquid assets outside the farm or business | Requires a large enough cushion to avoid touching operating capital |
| Hybrid life insurance with LTC rider | Clients who want a death benefit if care is never needed | Higher premiums than standalone LTC policies |

Most families use a blend. A veteran might combine a modest [VA Aid and Attendance benefit](https://www.va.gov/pension/aid-attendance-housebound/) with a smaller insurance policy and a dedicated savings account, spreading the risk across three sources.

## The VA Aid and Attendance benefit is underused

Veterans and surviving spouses who need help with daily activities like bathing, dressing, or managing medication may qualify for Aid and Attendance, an additional payment on top of a standard VA pension. It's not automatic. You have to apply and document net worth and medical need, and approval can take several months. For a veteran already receiving a VA pension, it can offset a meaningful chunk of care costs, often several hundred dollars to over $2,000 a month. Details are on the [VA Aid and Attendance and Housebound Allowance page](https://www.va.gov/pension/aid-attendance-housebound/).

Farmers and small business owners who served decades ago may not realize their service still qualifies them for support now.

## The tax side most people miss

Long-term care expenses can be deductible as medical expenses if they exceed 7.5% of adjusted gross income, and premiums for qualified long-term care insurance are deductible up to age-based limits set annually by the IRS. What counts is laid out in [IRS Publication 502](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p502).

This is where families lose money without realizing it. If a parent's care is paid partly by the family and partly by savings, someone needs to keep a clean record of what was spent, when, and by whom, throughout the year.

## Building a plan instead of reacting to a crisis

Families who handle this well start years before it's urgent. That usually means:

- Reviewing insurability in your mid-50s while premiums are still reasonable
- Checking veteran status and pension eligibility for Aid and Attendance
- Setting a savings target based on local facility costs, not national averages
- Coordinating any care spending with your annual tax planning so deductions aren't left on the table

For agribusiness families, land and equipment are often illiquid, so a care event can force a sale at the wrong time without a cash reserve. Planning this alongside your farm succession and retirement plan works best.

## Common Questions

**How much does long-term care actually cost in South Dakota?**

Assisted living typically runs $3,500 to $5,500 a month, while a private nursing home room can run $8,000 to $10,000. Costs vary by facility, so get current local quotes.

**Is long-term care insurance worth it if I'm already in my 60s?**

It can be, but premiums rise and underwriting tightens with age, so a hybrid policy with an LTC rider or self-funding may fit better. Review it with your CPA alongside your retirement projections.

**Can veterans get help paying for long-term care?**

Wartime veterans and surviving spouses who need help with daily activities may qualify for VA Aid and Attendance. Eligibility depends on service, medical need, and net worth, and applications can take months, so start early.

**Are long-term care expenses tax deductible?**

Some can be, once they exceed 7.5% of adjusted gross income, and certain LTC premiums are deductible up to IRS limits. Good records throughout the year make claiming them much easier.
