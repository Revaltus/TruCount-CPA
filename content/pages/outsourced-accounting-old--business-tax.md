## Tax preparation that gets you back to your business

Tax season shouldn't take over your calendar for three months and then disappear until next April. At TruCount CPA PC, tax preparation and planning run all year, not just in the weeks before a deadline. Farmers watching commodity prices, agribusiness operators managing input costs, and professional service business owners in Brookings all need a plan that adjusts as their numbers change, not a form filled out after the fact.

TruCount CPA PC was founded in 2009 to give South Dakota business owners a way to know their tax position before December 31, not after. When you know your numbers sooner, decisions get easier and surprises get rarer.

## Year-round tax planning instead of once-a-year filing

Most tax preparation happens backward: a business owner hands over their tax documents in March and finds out in April what they owe. That model leaves no room to change anything. TruCount CPA PC works the other direction. Tax planning happens throughout the year on a fixed monthly fee, bundled with the rest of your accounting work, so there's no bill that shows up out of nowhere in tax season.

Everything runs through a secure online platform, so you can see where your business stands anytime instead of waiting for a year-end phone call. Quarterly check-ins flag tax moves while there's still time to act on them, whether that's adjusting estimated payments, timing equipment purchases, or restructuring how income flows through your entities. By the time your return is due, there's nothing left to figure out. It's already been planned for, reviewed, and confirmed with you along the way.

## Specialized tax strategy for farmers and agribusiness

Farm tax returns carry rules that a general practice CPA might see once a year. TruCount CPA PC works with farmers and agribusiness operators across Brookings, SD, and the Midwest every season, which means these strategies get applied before December, not discovered after the return is filed.

- R&D credit review for qualifying farm activities, including seed trials, precision ag testing, and new equipment integration
- Excess fertility deduction analysis for purchased farmland with above-average soil nutrient levels
- Multi-entity structuring for operations that combine a farm with a trucking company, feed lot, or other related business
- Depreciation timing and Section 179 planning tied to equipment purchases
- Income averaging and self-employment tax planning for years with swings in farm income

These strategies save real money without inviting audit risk, because they're grounded in current IRS guidance for agricultural taxpayers, and they get revisited every year as your operation changes.

## Tax support built for service-based businesses

Consultants, architects, chiropractors and other professional service businesses in the Midwest face a different set of questions than a farm operation does. The biggest one is usually entity structure: is an S-corp saving money, or is it just adding payroll complexity without a real tax benefit? TruCount CPA PC runs the numbers on reasonable salary, distributions, and self-employment tax before recommending a structure, not after you've already filed as one.

Retirement plans get the same treatment. A SEP IRA, SIMPLE IRA, or 401(k) each carries different tax timing and contribution limits, and the right choice depends on payroll size and cash flow, not a generic recommendation. We walk through the tradeoffs in plain terms, connect the retirement decision to your entity structure, and revisit both whenever your business grows enough to change the math.
