## Check your federal and state refund status in one place

Wondering where your refund is? You don't need to dig through bookmarks or guess which government site to trust. The IRS "Where's My Refund?" tool at irs.gov tracks federal returns, and the South Dakota Department of Revenue handles state-level questions for filers in Brookings, Kingsbury, and Moody counties. TruCount CPA PC didn't build these tools, and we won't pretend we did, but we know how to read them and explain what they're telling you.

For federal refunds, visit irs.gov/refunds and enter your details directly. South Dakota has no state income tax on individuals, so most of our clients only need the federal tool, though business filers with multi-state activity may need to check other states' portals depending on where they operate. If a status message confuses you or looks stuck, that's exactly the kind of question our team answers every day during filing season.

## What to have ready before you check your refund

The IRS and state tools ask for the same basic information every time. Having it ready saves you a second trip back to the site.

- Your Social Security number or, for business entities, your EIN
- Your exact filing status (single, married filing jointly, head of household, etc.)
- The exact refund amount shown on your return, down to the dollar
- Your filing date, or an estimate if you're not sure
- Confirmation of whether you e-filed or mailed a paper return

Ag producers filing under a farm entity and service business owners filing under an LLC or S-corp should pull the EIN from the actual return, not a bank statement or prior-year document. A mismatched number is the most common reason these tools return an error instead of a status.

## Typical refund timelines for farm, business, and individual returns

Most e-filed individual returns with direct deposit see a refund in 21 days or less, according to the IRS. Paper returns take longer, often 6 to 8 weeks, and that gap widens during peak season in March and April.

Farm and agribusiness returns tend to move slower simply because they're more complex. Multi-entity operations, depreciation schedules, and returns claiming credits like the R&D credit for farm operations or the excess fertility deduction often get pulled for additional review before the refund releases. That's not a red flag on your return, it's standard IRS process for anything outside a simple wage-and-standard-deduction filing. Service-based business owners filing under an S-corp or partnership structure should expect similar timelines to other business entities rather than individual return speed.

## Why your refund may be delayed or different than expected

A refund that's smaller than expected, or later than expected, almost always has a specific cause. Credits under review, like agricultural R&D claims or excess fertility deductions, can add processing time while the IRS verifies documentation. A prior-year balance owed, even a small one, will reduce or offset this year's refund automatically.

Entity structure issues cause delays too. If a farm or service business changed its structure mid-year, from a sole proprietorship to an LLC or from a partnership to an S-corp, mismatched EINs between systems can stall processing. None of these are unusual, and none of them mean something went wrong with your return. They mean the IRS is doing exactly what it's supposed to do with a more complex filing.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Refund tracker

**Q: How do I check my federal tax refund status?**
A: Visit irs.gov and use the 'Where's My Refund?' tool. You'll need your Social Security number or EIN, filing status, and the exact refund amount from your return. Status updates typically appear within 24 hours of e-filing or four weeks after mailing a paper return.

**Q: Does South Dakota have a state refund tracker?**
A: South Dakota does not levy a state income tax on individuals, so most personal filers only need to check the federal IRS refund tool. Business filers with activity in other states may need to check that state's revenue department separately.

**Q: Why is my farm or business refund taking longer than expected?**
A: Multi-entity returns, depreciation schedules, and credits like the R&D credit or excess fertility deduction often trigger additional IRS review. This adds processing time but doesn't mean anything is wrong with your return.

**Q: Can TruCount CPA PC track my refund for me?**
A: We don't control IRS or state systems, but we prepared your return and know exactly what's in it. If your refund status looks off or delayed, we can explain why faster than a call to the IRS.
